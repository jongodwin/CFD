Model and assumptions

What is solved, and what is inferred
  • Flow. Incompressible Navier–Stokes with swirl on a 2-D axisymmetric (r, z) grid: van Leer advection, projection method with a direct pressure solve, mixing-length eddy viscosity and a power-law wall function. The liquid surface is flat and free-slip; the tank bottom is flat.
  • Impeller and baffles. Blades are a blade-element momentum source in the swept volume, so radial or axial pumping follows from blade angle and rotation. Baffles are a drag on swirl in the annulus they occupy. Blade-to-blade and baffle-to-baffle detail, trailing vortices and surface vortexing are averaged out. Power and pumping numbers come from the solved field and typically land within about 30% of published values.
  • Feed and outlet. In an axisymmetric model the feed is a ring at the liquid surface and a side outlet is a slot around the wall, so feed-jet momentum is understated.
  • RTD and age. A tracer step is transported on the frozen mean flow with turbulent diffusivity νt/0.8 (first-order upwind, implicit in time). The mean of the simulated distribution is checked against V/Q.
  • Solids. Particles are not tracked. Terminal velocity uses Schiller–Naumann drag, hindered settling uses Richardson–Zaki, and the concentration map is a drift-flux balance (settling against turbulent dispersion) at the stated loading. The just-suspended speed is Zwietering's correlation with a geometry constant S estimated from impeller type, D/T and clearance. Solids do not feed back on the liquid flow.
  • Use. Screening and comparison of design options. Confirm critical designs with 3-D CFD or tests.
