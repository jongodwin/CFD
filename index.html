How the model works

The kiln is divided into axial cells. Each cell holds a well-mixed slice of the bed and of the freeboard gas. Solids always move from the feed end to the discharge end; the gas moves with them (co-current) or against them (counter-current). For every cell the model solves energy balances for the bed, the gas, the inner wall and the steel shell, plus a molar balance for every species.

  • Radiation uses a grey-gas enclosure of gas, exposed wall and bed surface. Inside the flame the gas emissivity rises to the flame emissivity, so the flame radiates to the wall and the bed.
  • Burner heat is released along the flame length with the chosen profile; fuel and combustion air enter at the gas inlet end. Fuel is burnt on its lower heating value.
  • Convection between gas, wall and bed uses Tscheng & Watkinson, a pipe-flow correlation, or fixed coefficients. The covered wall passes heat to the bed by contact (penetration theory or a fixed coefficient). Chains or lifters add a volumetric gas–solids coefficient over a chosen zone.
  • Wall losses pass by conduction through the lining layers and leave the shell by convection (natural plus wind) and radiation.
  • Reactions follow Arrhenius kinetics, zero or first order in the key reactant mass fraction: dw/dt = −k·wⁿ with k = A·exp(−Ea/RT). Reaction heat is applied at 25 °C and species carry their sensible heat between phases, so the energy balance closes exactly.
  • Bed depth comes from the Kramers equation (slope, speed, angle of repose, discharge dam) or from a fixed fill fraction.

The steady state is found by marching a start-up transient with physical hold-ups to convergence with Newton's method. All enthalpies are referred to 25 °C.
